Build a weekly business report

Owners drown in data and still cannot answer “was this a good week?”. A weekly report picks four or five numbers that drive decisions, calculates them the same way every week and fits on one screen. You will choose the measures, write the formulas, test them against hand counts and design the one-page summary.

# Weekly report: a small grocery shop

## Measures
Decisions each week: what to restock, whether to stay open later on Fridays, which supplier to pay first.

- Sales this week vs last week · informs: whether the week was normal · formula: SUMIFS on the Amount column by week
- Top 10 items by quantity · informs: what to restock · formula: a pivot of Item by Quantity, top 10
- Sales after 18:00 on Friday · informs: whether to stay open later · formula: SUMIFS with a time condition
- Supplier bills due within 7 days · informs: who to pay first · formula: FILTER on the Bills tab by due date

Candidates I dropped, and why: "customers per day", because the notebook does not record it.

## Formulas
Data comes from: a sales notebook copied into a sheet each evening
```
=SUMIFS(Sales!C:C, Sales!A:A, ">="&B1, Sales!A:A, "<"&B1+7)
=SUMIFS(Sales!C:C, Sales!A:A, ">="&B1, Sales!B:B, ">=18:00", Sales!D:D, "Fri")
=FILTER(Bills!A:C, Bills!C:C <= TODAY()+7)
```

## QA tests
- Test 1: sales on Monday 3 November · hand count: 214.50 · formula: 214.50 · match
- Test 2: bread loaves sold this week · hand count: 96 · formula: 91 · fixed: five rows typed "Bread " with a space
- Test 3: a week with a public holiday · hand count: 0 for the holiday · formula: 0 · match

## One-page report
- Top line: sales this week, US$1,412, up 6% on last week
- Then: top 10 items, Friday late sales, bills due, each with an arrow up or down
- Last: one sentence, e.g. "Restock bread and cooking oil; pay the dairy supplier by Thursday"

## Boundary
- Every formula was checked against a hand count. Example numbers are invented.
- The report shows what happened. It does not predict sales.

    Pro moves
    • If nobody would act differently when a number changes, drop it.
    • Test with a week that has a public holiday or a missing day.
    • Always compare with last week. A number alone means little.

    Questions people ask
    Which spreadsheet should I use?
    Any free one: Google Sheets, LibreOffice Calc or Excel online. The formulas here use functions they all share.
    Should the report forecast next week?
    Not at first. Report what happened accurately for a month before trying to predict anything, and label any forecast clearly as a guess.
    Will this make me money?
    Nobody can promise that, and Open Practice never does. This workshop helps you make work that is worth paying for and keep the evidence. Whether anyone buys depends on your market, your outreach and your work.
    Do I need an AI key to use this?
    No. The guided template teaches the same skill with no AI at all. If you add your own free key (Groq, Google Gemini, OpenRouter, Mistral or Cerebras), the tool can also draft and revise. The key stays in your browser and requests go straight to that provider.
    Where is my work saved?
    In this browser only (local storage), until you copy or download it. Nothing you type is sent to Open Practice.
